# Crit 8 reflection — It's alive!

**The breakthrough that moved the work forward:** I didn't come in with an
app idea. The agent offered a spread of examples across categories — social
space, game, instrument, tool, satire — small and specific rather than a
generic "multi-user real-time app." Having concrete options in front of me,
instead of a blank page, is what let me actually pick something I wanted:
an anonymous cross-course vent wall with a resonance count. The scoping
decisions after that — any course rather than one, permanent rather than
expiring, sorted by resonance — were mine, but I only got to make them once
I had a real example to react to instead of an abstract brief.

**What this changed about who I want to be as a developer:** this week I
spent almost no time writing code myself, and most of my time on the
decisions that actually shaped the app: which idea to build, what "good"
means for it, what to defer. I came out of it feeling like I was acting more
as a decision-maker than as "the person who writes the code" — the agent
handled the implementation, but the choices that make this app mine rather
than a generic template (who it's for, what it promises, what it refuses to
do) were ones only I could make. That's a different relationship to the
work than I'm used to, and one I want to keep deliberately: direct the
judgment calls, verify what gets built against them, rather than disappear
into typing.
